# SELDON alert consumer: first live night (2026-11-13)

**Summary.** On its first full Rubin night, the SELDON alert consumer
forecast every detection it owed a forecast, with no intake errors. 98.5% of
forecasts were stored within 10 minutes of the triggering broker publishing
the alert, and the 90th percentile was 3.5 minutes. Fink and Babamul worked
as expected. Our Lasair filter did not stream during the night, so Lasair's
live latency and volume are still unmeasured.

- **Night:** Rubin dayObs 2026-11-13 (12:00 UTC on 11-13 to 12:00 UTC on 11-14).
- **Reports run:** 2026-11-14 at 12:40 UTC.
- **Service version:** `777111a`, the merge of scimma/seldon-service#10.
- **Deployment:** the development stack on one host, with the consumer limited to one CPU and one torch thread.

## What was tested

The consumer reads Rubin alerts from three brokers:

| Broker | Role | Stream |
|---|---|---|
| Fink | Qualifying | topic `fink_most_likely_sn_lsst` |
| Lasair | Qualifying | the service's own filter (host-context class `SN` or `NT`, reliability >= 0.6) |
| Babamul | Full stream | every `babamul.lsst.*` topic |

**When a detection is forecast:**
- An object qualifies when a qualifying broker reports a detection that passes that broker's reliability cut and SN-like threshold.
- From then on, every detection of the object is forecast once with SELDON, whichever broker delivers it.
- Each forecast is stored in Postgres.

**What the plan asked of this check:**
- 90% of forecasts stored within 10 minutes of the triggering broker publishing the alert.
- Every owed detection accounted for.
- Any gaps and intake failures visible in the reports.

## Results

### Latency

**How it is measured:** from the triggering broker's publication of the alert to the stored forecast.

| Triggering broker | Forecasts | 90th percentile | Within 10 min |
|---|---|---|---|
| All | 963 | 3.5 min | 949 (98.5%) |
| Fink | 787 | 2.0 min | 787 (100%) |
| Babamul | 176 | 7.1 min | 162 (92.0%) |

**No forecast was left out of the figures:** none was a replay, and none was missing a publish time or was a re-forecast.

**The 10-minute target was met.**

**All 14 forecasts over 10 minutes were triggered by Babamul.** They have not yet been examined one by one. The likely cause is a known overstatement in how latency is measured:
- a later detection is timed from its first delivery;
- when Babamul delivered that detection before its object qualified, the measured latency also includes the wait for qualification.

### Coverage

| Broker | Detections owed a forecast | Forecast | Refused | Missed | Still owed | Covered |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Babamul | 963 | 963 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 100% |
| Fink | 810 | 810 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 100% |
| All (each detection once) | 963 | 963 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 100% |

**Every detection owed a forecast received one.** Babamul delivered every one of them, including all 810 that Fink delivered.

**Alerts arrived only from 00:16 to 06:43 UTC.** That makes this a shorter night than the one measured during the broker probe (2026-11-12), when Fink sent 3,390 alerts.

**Forecasting capacity was not tested.** The forecast load was well below one process's estimated capacity, so this night does not test the CPU headroom.

### Gaps

**What the consumer records:** a *suspected* gap whenever a broker sends nothing for more than 60 minutes during the night window, which runs from 23:00 to 11:00 UTC.

**What it recorded:**
- Fink: three suspected gaps.
- Babamul: three suspected gaps.
- Lasair: one suspected gap.
- No *confirmed* gap, meaning a gap where a broker dropped alerts before the consumer read them.

**Each recorded gap lasts exactly 1h00m.** That is by design: a suspected gap is stored when the silence is detected, so the recorded end is that moment, not when deliveries resumed. The time between consecutive deliveries gives the actual quiet periods:

| Quiet period (UTC) | Fink | Babamul |
|---|---|---|
| Start of night window to the first alert | 23:00 - 00:21 | 23:00 - 00:16 |
| Mid-night | 01:22 - 03:24 (2h02m) | 01:58 - 03:21 (1h23m) |
| Mid-night | 03:25 - 04:21 (55 min) | 03:26 - 04:18 (52 min) |
| Late night | 05:37 - 06:16 (39 min) | 05:35 - 06:14 (39 min) |
| End | nothing after 06:39 | nothing after 06:43 |

**These pauses came from upstream, not from the consumer.** Two independent brokers went quiet and resumed at the same times, which points to pauses in Rubin's alert production, such as observing interruptions.

## Lasair: the filter did not stream

**Lasair was working that night.** The SCiMMA crossmatch service received 8,939 objects from Lasair for the same night.

**Our filter's topic held only 10 messages:**
- All 10 were written together at 19:48 UTC on 11-13, before the night began and before the consumer started.
- Nothing was added during the night.

**The consumer was not at fault:** it was subscribed to all eight partitions of the topic throughout.

**The data format is right:**
- The 10 messages are full-alert JSON, with class `SN` and reliability of 0.6 or more.
- So the filter's output matches what the consumer's parser expects.

**Most likely cause:** the filter ran once, probably when it was saved, and is not set to stream new matches to Kafka.

**Next step:** check the filter's settings on lasair.lsst.ac.uk. No change to the service is needed; the consumer will start reading as soon as messages arrive.

**Until then, Lasair's live latency and nightly volume are still unmeasured.** This night's results reflect Fink as the only qualifying broker, with Babamul as the full stream.

## Follow-ups

1. **Lasair:** enable streaming on the filter, then measure its live latency and volume on the next night.
2. **Latency outliers:** examine the 14 Babamul-triggered forecasts over 10 minutes, to confirm they come from the known qualification-wait overstatement.
3. **Gap end times:** close a suspected gap when the broker's next delivery arrives, so the gaps report shows how long the silence actually lasted. This would change only how gaps are reported.
4. **Capacity:** repeat the check on a full, uninterrupted night to see the consumer under peak load.

## How these figures were produced

The figures come from the service's own read-only report command, run inside the stack:

```console
python manage.py alert_report latency --percentile 90 --threshold-minutes 10
python manage.py alert_report coverage --from-night 2026-11-13 --to-night 2026-11-13
python manage.py alert_report gaps
python manage.py alert_report skips --from-night 2026-11-13
```

The other figures came from elsewhere:

- **Delivery counts and quiet periods:** queried directly from the service database.
- **The Lasair topic's contents:** read from its Kafka offsets with a separate, read-only consumer group.
- **Lasair's object count for the night:** from the SCiMMA crossmatch service.
