# SELDON alert consumer: second live night (2026-11-14)

**Summary.** On the second live night, the service's Lasair filter streamed
for the first time.

- **Lasair worked well.** It became the main qualifying broker, qualifying 3,846 objects to Fink's 1,174. Every Lasair-triggered forecast was stored within 10 minutes of Lasair publishing the alert.
- **Coverage was complete.** Every detection owed a forecast received one, and no alerts were skipped.
- **The consumer could not keep up with Babamul.** Babamul sent almost twice the alerts of any night measured so far. The consumer read them at about 9 a second, fell up to three hours behind, and the delay spread to Fink.
- **Forecasting was not the bottleneck.** The cost is reading and storing each alert's light curve.

**What this means for the targets:** the 10-minute target still holds overall, but not for forecasts triggered by Fink.

- **Night:** Rubin dayObs 2026-11-14 (12:00 UTC on 11-14 to 12:00 UTC on 11-15).
- **Reports run:** 2026-11-15 at about 09:40 UTC.
- **Service version:** `777111a`, the merge of scimma/seldon-service#10.
- **Deployment:** the development stack on one host, with the consumer limited to one CPU and one torch thread.
- **Previous night:** `docs/reports/2026-11-14-live-check-night-2026-11-13.md`.

## Background

The consumer reads Rubin alerts from three brokers:

| Broker | Role | Stream |
|---|---|---|
| Fink | Qualifying | topic `fink_most_likely_sn_lsst` |
| Lasair | Qualifying | the service's own filter (host-context class `SN` or `NT`, latest reliability >= 0.6, full-alert output) |
| Babamul | Full stream | every `babamul.lsst.*` topic |

**When a detection is forecast:**
- An object qualifies when a qualifying broker reports a detection that passes that broker's reliability cut and SN-like threshold.
- From then on, every detection of the object is forecast once with SELDON, whichever broker delivers it.

**The plan's latency target:** 90% of forecasts stored within 10 minutes of the triggering broker publishing the alert.

**The Lasair filter was fixed on 2026-11-14:**
- It had not streamed on the first night, and was corrected on 11-14.
- Lasair recreated the filter's Kafka topic when the filter was saved. The consumer then skipped 6 of the 10 messages Lasair wrote at that moment, because its saved read positions belonged to the old topic.
- Those 6 are the only Lasair messages not read. The issue is listed under follow-ups.

**Lasair is now the main qualifying broker:**
- **Qualifications:** of the objects that qualified this night, 3,846 qualified through Lasair and 1,174 through Fink.
- **Little overlap:** the two brokers select mostly different objects. Only 501 objects were delivered by both, out of 3,905 from Lasair and 1,895 from Fink.
- **Order of publication:** on the 619 detections that both brokers published, Lasair was ahead of Fink by a median of 0.2 minutes (p90 1.0 min). Babamul published every one of the 4,864 Lasair detections first, a median of 1.8 minutes before Lasair (p90 3.3 min).

**Lasair's alerts came in one burst.** 4,733 of them arrived in the hour from 00:00 UTC, and only a trickle followed until 04:17. Fink was concentrated in the same hour (1,824 of its 2,250 alerts), so the burst reflects what Rubin observed early in the night. The suspected Lasair gaps later in the night are hours when the filter matched nothing. The topic's offsets show no lost messages.

| Broker | Broker publication to consumer receipt |

The hourly receipt counts show where the limit is:

| Hour (UTC) | Babamul | Fink | Lasair |
|---|---|---|---|
| 00 | 8,177 | 1,824 | 4,733 |
| 01 | 32,200 | 327 | 0 |
| 02 | 33,200 | 12 | 25 |
| 03 | 32,164 | 11 | 19 |
| 04 | 1,985 | 65 | 87 |
| 05 | 241 | 11 | 0 |

**Why it fell behind:**
- **The consumer's intake ceiling:** from 01:00 to 04:00 UTC it took in an almost constant 32,000 to 33,000 Babamul alerts an hour, about 9 a second. That flat rate is the limit of the single consumer process.
- **Babamul published faster than that:** its alerts were published a median of 2.7 minutes after observation, but reached the consumer a median of 106 minutes after publication. The backlog cleared at about 05:36.
- **Forecasting is not the limit.** 6,433 forecasts at 0.1 to 0.45 seconds each is under an hour of work over the night. The cost is in reading each alert and storing its light-curve points.

**The effect on the other brokers:**
- **Fink:** all brokers are read in one loop, so Fink's alerts also waited.
- **Lasair:** it was hardly affected, because most of its alerts arrived in the first hour, before the Babamul backlog built up.

**No forecasts were lost, but latency suffered:**
- **Replays:** 152 Babamul deliveries arrived more than 60 minutes after publication. They count as replays, and the latency figure leaves them out.

**The plan's target, 90% within 10 minutes, still holds overall but not for Fink.** A busier night, or a qualifying burst arriving during a Babamul backlog, would lower the overall figure too.

### Gaps

Each broker recorded suspected gaps, meaning more than 60 minutes without a delivery during the night window of 23:00 to 11:00 UTC. No *confirmed* gap was recorded; a confirmed gap means a broker dropped alerts before the consumer read them.

**A recorded gap's end time means less than it seems:**
- A suspected gap is stored when the silence is detected, so the recorded end is that moment.
- It is not when deliveries resumed, so every recorded gap reads as exactly 1h00m long.

**What the gaps show this night:**
- **Gaps on all brokers at once:** the start of the night window (23:00 to about 00:20) and the end of the night, after 05:36 to 05:38. Rubin was not producing alerts then.
- **Gaps on one broker only:** Lasair's mid-night gaps, which are hours when the filter matched nothing.

## Follow-ups

1. **Consumer throughput.** This is the most important follow-up. Raise intake well above 9 alerts a second, so a busy night cannot delay the qualifying brokers. Options:
   - **Separate process:** read the full stream in its own consumer process.
   - **Cheaper storage:** make storing each Babamul alert cheaper, for example by inserting in batches.
   - **Fewer points stored:** keep light-curve points only for objects that could still qualify.
2. **Recreated topics.** When a stored read position is beyond the end of a partition (as after Lasair recreates a filter's topic), restart that partition from its earliest message, and record a confirmed gap instead of skipping silently.
3. **Gap end times.** Close a suspected gap when the broker's next delivery arrives, so the report shows the real length of the silence.
4. **Storage.** Delete the light-curve points of objects still unqualified after the seven-day hold. After the first night, these points were about 82% of the database. This night's much larger Babamul volume makes the cleanup more pressing.
5. **Per-night latency.** Add a night option to the latency report, so each night can be reported on its own.

## How these figures were produced

**From the service's own read-only report command**, run inside the stack:

```console
python manage.py alert_report coverage --from-night 2026-11-14 --to-night 2026-11-14
python manage.py alert_report latency --percentile 90 --threshold-minutes 10
python manage.py alert_report gaps
python manage.py alert_report skips --from-night 2026-11-14
```

**From the service database, queried directly:**
- **Scope:** deliveries received between 12:00 UTC on 11-14 and 12:00 UTC on 11-15.
- **Figures:**
  - delivery counts and hourly volume;
  - time from publication to receipt;
  - time from observation to publication;
  - shared detections between brokers;
  - qualifications per broker.
- **Converting observation times:** Rubin's MJD (TAI) was converted to UTC by subtracting 37 seconds.

**The Lasair topic's offsets** were read directly from Kafka.
